# OKX Wire Transfer: What a USD Withdrawal Costs, How Long It Takes, and Why the Option Is Missing From Your Account (US vs International Wires Explained)

If you're a verified U.S. user in an eligible state, a domestic wire is how you move larger USD amounts from OKX to your bank. OKX charges **$30** for the withdrawal, the request can sit in Pending for up to 3 business days, and the money typically reaches your bank 1–3 business days after it leaves the platform. International wires run on different rules: OKX's help page lists them for individuals residing in eligible EU states, funds take up to 5 business days after leaving OKX, and the fee is whatever your payment method page shows rather than a published number.

What follows is the part most quick answers skip: the deposit you have to make before your first withdrawal, why a newly added bank account needs to be verified with your own money, and what happens to the $30 when a wire bounces back.

> **Short version:** U.S. domestic wire if you're in the U.S. and moving a larger amount, $30 flat, 1–3 business days after the wire leaves OKX, no cancellation once confirmed. International wire if you're an eligible EU resident, fee shown in-app, up to 5 business days. Neither is available everywhere, so check what your own account actually displays.

## U.S. domestic wire: three conditions before you can send anything

The withdrawal button isn't the starting point. Three things have to be true first.

**Your account is verified and you live in an eligible state.** OKX restricts USD withdrawals to verified U.S. customers in states where the service is offered. If you're verified but the feature still isn't there, the state restriction is the usual reason.

**You've made at least one successful deposit.** OKX states plainly that you must have made a successful deposit before you can make a withdrawal. A fresh account with a zero deposit history won't let you wire out, even if the balance came from somewhere else.

**The receiving account is verified as yours.** For any newly added bank account, you confirm ownership by making a USD deposit into OKX from that same bank account. Once the deposit lands in your OKX account, the bank account is verified. There's no document upload shortcut here.

That last point catches people who try to send to a spouse's account, a business account, or a newly opened savings account. The name on your OKX account has to match the name on the receiving bank account. Mismatches are one of the most common reasons wires get returned, and a returned wire costs you the fee.

### Web

1. Log in at app.okx.com, go to **Assets**, then **Withdraw > US Dollar**.
2. Pick **Domestic wire transfer**, choose the receiving account, click **Next**.
3. Enter the amount and select **Preview**.
4. Check the account and the amount on the preview screen, complete security verification (SMS code, authenticator or passkey), then select **Withdraw**.

### App

1. Open the OKX app, tap **Transfer**, then **Withdraw**.
2. Under **Cash**, select **US Dollar**.
3. Choose **Domestic wire transfer** and the receiving account.
4. Enter the amount, tap **Preview**, review, then enter your authentication or SMS code and confirm.

The preview screen is the last chance to catch a wrong account number. Once you confirm, the transfer is final.

## What the $30 actually covers, and where the rest of the money goes

The amount that arrives is your withdrawal amount minus the OKX fee. Your receiving bank can add its own incoming wire charge on top, and OKX has no control over that. On a $1,000 withdrawal, budget for $970 before your bank takes its cut, not $1,000.

Timing has two stages, and people mix them up when they're checking their statement. OKX may take up to 3 business days to process the request, during which the transaction shows as Pending and nothing moves on weekends or U.S. banking holidays. After the funds leave OKX, you get an email confirming the send, and the wire generally lands within 1–3 business days depending on your bank. So a Friday afternoon request can legitimately take the better part of a week to appear.

You cannot cancel, recall or edit a domestic wire once confirmed. OKX Support can't pull it back either. If the receiving bank rejects or returns it, the money comes back to your OKX account automatically.

## International wire: region-gated, and the fee isn't published

OKX's international wire page states the method is only available to individuals residing in eligible EU states. Pages for other regions exist in OKX's help centre with their own eligibility wording, which tells you the feature is opened market by market rather than globally. Don't assume it works from anywhere, and don't assume the EU page describes your account either.

If you can't see "Withdraw USD" at all, OKX's own explanation is regulatory: cash withdrawals are only available in specific regions. It's a regional restriction, not a broken account, and support can't switch it on for you.

The flow itself is short:

- **Web:** select **Cash** and **USD balance** to start a withdrawal, choose **International wire transfer**, click **Next**, enter the amount, select **Preview withdrawal**, enter your authentication or SMS code, then **Withdraw**.
- **App:** **Transfer > Withdraw**, select **USD** and **international wire transfer**, **Next**, enter the amount, **Preview withdrawal**, **Withdraw**, then enter your code and **Confirm**.

The same ownership rule applies. A newly added receiving account has to be verified with a cash deposit from that same account before you can withdraw to it, and you can't withdraw while verification is still pending. Withdrawal history sits under Cash withdrawals on the cash withdrawal page.

On cost, OKX points to the limits and fees shown on the payment method itself rather than a fixed schedule. Read that number before you confirm, because it isn't the $30 U.S. figure and it isn't the same for every account.

## When a wire comes back: the $30 doesn't

This is the part that costs people real money, so it's worth reading before you send rather than after.

A receiving bank sends a wire back when it can't credit the funds. OKX lists five common causes:

- The bank details on your linked account are outdated, or the account is closed, frozen or restricted.
- The name on your OKX account doesn't match the name on the receiving bank account.
- Your bank doesn't accept incoming wires to the account OKX paid out to.
- The ABA routing number used doesn't support wire transfers.
- Your bank flagged the transfer during its own compliance or risk checks.

Your original withdrawal will show as Completed in your history, and the returned amount is credited back to your OKX USD balance as a new deposit. Settlement can take up to **10 business days** from the date your bank returns the wire.

Three things hurt here. The credited amount may be lower than what you withdrew. The **$30 OKX wire fee is not refunded**, because the transfer was already sent. And your receiving bank or an intermediary bank may deduct its own return-handling fee before the funds reach OKX, a charge OKX neither sets nor receives.

Before retrying, confirm your bank details and account status with your bank, then re-verify the receiving account with a domestic wire deposit from that same account, then submit a new withdrawal.

## If the money hasn't arrived after three business days

Domestic wires normally land 1–3 business days after leaving OKX. Past three business days, work through this in order:

1. Open the cash withdrawal page and check the status under **All withdrawals**.
2. Confirm you received the email saying the funds were sent from OKX.
3. Call your bank first and ask them to search for the incoming wire using the reference number from that email. Funds sometimes sit unposted at the receiving bank.
4. If your bank can't find it, contact OKX Support and request a wire trace.

Have this ready so the trace doesn't stall: your OKX withdrawal ID or the reference number from the send email, the receiving bank's name, the last four digits of the account and routing number, a bank statement or screenshot covering the send date through today showing no credit, and any return or rejection notice from your bank. A trace with OKX's banking partner takes up to 3 business days.

## Depositing by wire: the details you have to copy correctly

For a U.S. domestic wire deposit, OKX gives you the transfer details to enter in your own online banking: recipient name, recipient address, the ABA routing number and the account number. Copy them exactly and re-check them inside your bank's app before sending. A single transposed digit in the routing number is enough to send the wire into a return.

For an international wire deposit, confirm the amount sits within your available limit before you submit, then open **Deposit**, select **USD** and **international wire transfer**, and tap **Next** to see the details you need to copy. The deposit isn't just funding your balance, it's also what verifies that bank account for future withdrawals.

## Which one applies to you

- **U.S., verified, eligible state, moving a larger amount:** domestic wire. $30, 1–3 business days after the send, plan around weekends and banking holidays.
- **Eligible EU resident:** international wire. Up to 5 business days after the send, and read the fee on the payment method before confirming.
- **Neither applies:** check what your account actually offers before planning around wire transfers at all. Regional availability decides this, not preference.

Two checks before you hit Withdraw: the name on the receiving bank account matches your OKX name exactly, and the account number and amount on the Preview screen are what you intend. Neither can be fixed after the fact.
